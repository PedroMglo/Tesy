# M3 measured target cache-capacity intervention

Objective and frozen hypothesis: `research/M3-CAPACITY-PREFLIGHT-20260926.md`, based on lab commit `364ecf4`. Increase only the existing cache from 16 to 24 slots/layer on original candidate `1248fd8fa8cfebaece5ea992e4d951c1e18bb9d5`, keeping verified GPT-OSS 120B MXFP4 GGUF, routing/top-4, quantization, GPU8 placement, context4096, batch256/microbatch32, O_DIRECT, no preload/repack, greedy sampler, workload and 18 GiB/zero-swap limit fixed. This is intervention 3/6 cumulative. No source optimization was made. Full per-run commands, hashes, cgroup conditions and samples are in the six JSON manifests and `results/m3-ab-summary.json`; raw stdout/stderr/samples remain local and untracked.

## Measured result

Three alternating A/B pairs, same 84-token then 201-token prompt workload and the same 158 then 114 decoded tokens in every run:

| Slots | Decode aggregate tok/s, runs 1–3 | Mean decode tok/s | Mean end-to-end s | Mean sampled process read GB | GPU used MiB | Peak cgroup GB range |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 16 | 2.127, 2.086, 2.124 | 2.112 | 295.887 | 398.289 | 2406 | 8.55–9.63 |
| 24 | 2.671, 2.633, 2.553 | 2.619 | 226.701 | 321.407 | 3112–3123 | 11.50–11.97 |

The observed mean decode increase was 24.0%; mean end-to-end time was 23.4% lower, and sampled process `read_bytes` 19.3% lower. These are workload-specific measurements, not a physical NVMe traffic attribution or statistical generalization. The first request's 24-slot rates were 2.928, 2.818, 2.734 tok/s; the second's were 2.382, 2.414, 2.338 tok/s. Every 24-slot request exceeded the strict 2.000 threshold over its short decode segment. A 15-minute sustained test remains a separate gate.

All six runs exited normally, had zero swap and zero cgroup max/OOM events, and generated byte-identical answer texts. The first answer passed arithmetic. The second answer preserved the known extraction failure: `day` is a full date instead of the required day number. The original plain-reference CPU four-token comparison remains 201,088/201,088 bitwise equal; a new production GPU8-placement four-token 16-vs-24 probe was also 201,088/201,088 bitwise equal, maximum absolute difference 0. The GPU8 comparison does not replace a plain GPU8 reference or prove all long-decode routed states. No slot identity, weights, routing or sampler changed by source inspection; a changed cache capacity only alters residency and misses.

Resource tradeoff: nominal encoded cache increases by 3.807 GB total, split as +2.961 GB for host layer buffers and +0.846 GB for GPU layer buffers. Observed GPU used increased by 706–717 MiB; sampled cgroup peaks varied with file accounting and remained below the 18 GiB cap. The `read_bytes` comparison is a process counter sampled just before exit, not exclusive device traffic. Per-tier hits, H2D bytes and exclusive NVMe bytes were not newly measured for 24 slots; lower read bytes plus identical work supports a cache-capacity explanation, with remaining attribution uncertainty. Existing 16-slot telemetry in `research/M2-RESULT-20260926.md` remains the mechanism observation.

Decision: retain 24 slots as the best validated bounded configuration for the target, with original 16-slot script and results preserved. M3_MEASURED_OPTIMIZATION is demonstrated for this workload and bounded correctness coverage. M5_PERFORMANCE_TARGET_MET and M6_LARGER_MODEL_TASK_ADVANTAGE remain unproven. No alternatives were implemented or reverted; alternative interventions considered are documented in the preflight. Next discriminating gate: the frozen 8-request sustained run in `research/M5-PREFLIGHT-20260926.md`, followed by a localhost server TTFT/task check if resources remain safe.
