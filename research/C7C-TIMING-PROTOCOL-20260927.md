Objective: test the refutable hypothesis that native P12 placement saves completed work at 113/496 prompt IDs plus the same 32 teacher-forced continuation IDs. The alternative is that extra GPU residency, transfer or decode cost cancels prefill savings. The exact executable rules, source hashes, inputs, options, pair orders and bounds are in `results/c7c-20260927T1224Z/timing-protocol.json`. P8 C4↔C7 bridge and P12 observer/canonical gates passed before this freeze. This is a profile comparison; routing may differ and I/O deltas will not be called a pure compute effect.

The primary metric is completed prefill plus all 32 step completions, excluding model load. Gain is calculated per pair as `100*(P8-P12)/P8`, then the median of those gains is used for work, prefill and TPOT. The first 113 screen can be neutral or negative without vetoing the 496 screen if numerical/resource gates hold. A 496 general gain requires the frozen 5% two-pair screen, then three new confirmation pairs with an 8% median rule. Before any 496 outputs, the protocol also freezes a narrow 1522 prefill specialization trigger: medium median prefill gain at least 3% and at least two percentage points above short. That later path still needs its own new screen and confirmation.

Cache condition is fresh expert pools, page cache uncontrolled, O_DIRECT observed. The 32 fixed IDs make decode trajectories comparable but are not generated tokens or quality evidence. Two paired runs cannot certify tails or sustained use. Expected benefit remains a hypothesis; costs include up to four full-model starts at 113 and four at 496, bounded by the global physical budget.
